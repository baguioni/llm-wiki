# Deep Memory Retrieval (DMR)

## Setup

- **Origin.** The MemGPT paper introduces the "deep memory retrieval" (DMR) task, based on the [Multi-Session Chat (MSC)](../datasets/multi-session-chat.md) dataset, to test the consistency of a conversational agent.[^packerMemGPTLLMsOperating2024]
- **Task.** The agent is asked a question by the user that explicitly refers back to a prior conversation (sessions 1–5) and has a very narrow expected answer range.[^packerMemGPTLLMsOperating2024]
- **Data.** Each multi-session chat in MSC has five sessions, and each session has roughly a dozen messages. The authors add a session 6 with a single question-answer pair between the same two personas. The question-answer pairs were generated by a separate LLM, instructed to write a question that could only be answered correctly using knowledge gained from the past sessions.[^packerMemGPTLLMsOperating2024]
- **Size.** The Zep paper describes the evaluation as 500 multi-session conversations, a subset of Multi-Session Chat, each with 5 chat sessions of up to 12 messages per session and one question/answer pair.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]
- **Metric.** Accuracy from an "LLM judge" ([LLM-as-a-judge](../methods/llm-as-a-judge.md)) that evaluates whether the generated response is consistent with the gold response, and ROUGE-L recall (R), chosen because the generated replies are more verbose than the gold answers.[^packerMemGPTLLMsOperating2024] The Zep paper reports only the judge accuracy: an LLM judge compares the agent's responses with the provided golden answers.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]
- **MemGPT protocol.** The baselines see a lossy summarization of the past five conversations. [MemGPT](../methods/memgpt.md) has access to the full conversation history but must reach it through paginated search queries.[^packerMemGPTLLMsOperating2024]
- **Zep protocol.** The conversations are ingested into Zep, and its search functions retrieve the top 10 most relevant nodes and edges. The DMR evaluation is also run with gpt-4-turbo-2024-04-09 for direct comparability with MemGPT's results.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]

## Current SOTA

- The best score among the wiki's papers is 98.2% for Zep with gpt-4o-mini.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]
- With the model matched to MemGPT (gpt-4-turbo), the best score is 94.8% for Zep.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]

## Superseded claims

- **Superseded.** MemGPT with GPT-4 Turbo reaches 93.4% accuracy and 0.827 ROUGE-L (R), the best row of the MemGPT paper's table.[^packerMemGPTLLMsOperating2024] The Zep paper states that MemGPT led with 93.4% using gpt-4-turbo, then reports 94.8% for Zep with the same model and 98.2% with gpt-4o-mini.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]

## Caveats

- **Small margins.** The Zep authors describe their gains over MemGPT and over the full-conversation baselines as marginal.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]
- **Short conversations.** Each conversation contains only 60 messages, which fits within current LLM context windows.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]
- **Different chat models.** The 98.2% and 98.0% rows use gpt-4o-mini, and no MemGPT number exists for that model. The Zep authors were unable to reproduce MemGPT's results with gpt-4o-mini, which they attribute to insufficient methodological details in the published work.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]
- **Judge.** The MemGPT judge prompt tells the judge to be generous: an answer that touches on the same topic as the gold answer is counted as CORRECT.[^packerMemGPTLLMsOperating2024] The Zep paper does not state that it uses the same judge prompt or judge model.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]
- **Generated questions.** The question-answer pairs were written by an LLM.[^packerMemGPTLLMsOperating2024]
- **Retrieval size in Zep.** The Zep experimental setup states that the 20 most relevant edges and entity nodes are retrieved, while its DMR section states the top 10 most relevant nodes and edges.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]
- **Size.** The 500-conversation figure comes from the Zep paper.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]

# Contradictions

**Does DMR show that a memory system is needed?** The two papers agree on every shared number, and they disagree on what the benchmark shows.

- The MemGPT paper states that MemGPT significantly outperforms the fixed-context baselines, with 93.4% against 35.3% for GPT-4 Turbo.[^packerMemGPTLLMsOperating2024]
- The Zep paper reports a full-conversation baseline at 94.4% with gpt-4-turbo, which slightly surpasses MemGPT's reported 93.4%, and 98.0% with gpt-4o-mini. The authors conclude that the high performance of simple full-context approaches highlights the benchmark's inadequacy for evaluating memory systems.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]
- The Zep paper also criticizes the design: the evaluation relies only on single-turn, fact-retrieval questions, many questions have ambiguous phrasing, and the dataset poorly represents real-world enterprise use cases.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]

The setting difference explains the gap. The MemGPT baselines see only a lossy summarization of the past five conversations.[^packerMemGPTLLMsOperating2024] The Zep full-conversation baseline puts the whole conversation in the context.[^rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025] The 35.3% baseline and the 94.4% baseline are therefore different baselines, both run with GPT-4 Turbo, and not two measurements of the same thing.
