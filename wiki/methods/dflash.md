# DFlash

## Overview

DFlash is a [speculative decoding](../methods/speculative-decoding.md) framework whose drafter is a lightweight block diffusion model. It generates all draft tokens of a block in a single forward pass and conditions the drafter on context features extracted from the target model.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026] Its authors report over 6× lossless acceleration across a range of models and tasks, and up to 2.5× higher speedup than [EAGLE-3](../methods/eagle-3.md).[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]

Two later papers in this wiki re-measure DFlash as a baseline: the DSpark paper, which retrains it, and the DPara paper.[^chengDSparkConfidenceScheduledSpeculative2026][^liuWhenParallelDrafter2026] Their numbers are recorded under "Papers using it", and the disagreements under "Contradictions".

## How it works

- **Cost model.** Per-token latency is $L=\frac{T_{\text{draft}}+T_{\text{verify}}}{\tau}$. An autoregressive drafter pays $T_{\text{draft}}=\gamma\cdot t_{\text{step}}$. A diffusion drafter pays $T_{\text{draft}}=t_{\text{parallel}}$, which is largely insensitive to $\gamma$ for moderate block sizes, so the drafter can be deeper.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- **Target context features.** During the prefill pass, hidden states from a fixed set of target layers, uniformly sampled from shallow to deep, are concatenated and passed through a lightweight projection layer into a compact target context feature.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- **[KV injection](../methods/kv-injection.md).** The fused feature is injected into the Key and Value projections of every draft layer, stored in the drafter's [KV cache](../methods/kv-cache.md), and reused across drafting iterations.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- **Parallel drafting.** All masked positions within a block are decoded in parallel in a single forward pass.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- **Training.** Anchor tokens are sampled at random from the response. Each anchor is the first position of a block, and the drafter predicts the next $\text{block\_size}-1$ tokens in parallel. All blocks are trained in one pass with a sparse attention mask. Cross-entropy at position $k$ is weighted by $w_{k}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{k-1}{\gamma}\right)$. The embedding layer and LM head are shared with the target and frozen.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- **Default configuration in the original paper.** 5 draft layers (8 for Qwen3 Coder), block size 16 (10 for LLaMA 3.1), target features from 5 layers. Training data is a mixture of around 800K samples from NVIDIA Nemotron Post-Training Dataset V2 and CodeAlpaca, with responses regenerated by the target model.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]

The DSpark paper describes the DFlash input as the embedding of an anchor token followed by $\gamma$ mask token embeddings, with logits produced for all mask positions.[^chengDSparkConfidenceScheduledSpeculative2026] The DPara paper describes DFlash under the serial schedule as anchoring on a single token (the bonus, which has no target feature of its own) appended to a fully featured prefix.[^liuWhenParallelDrafter2026]

## Variants

- **DFlash-AR.** An autoregressive drafter with KV injection, used in the original paper's ablation of KV injection against input fusion.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- **DFlash with input fusion.** The same block diffusion drafter with target features injected only at the input layer, as in EAGLE-3.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- **Long-context DFlash.** The base Qwen3.5-27B drafter, trained on 4K context, fine-tuned with 1.6K samples from LongAlign-10K for 3 epochs.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- **DFlash as the [DSpark](../methods/dspark.md) backbone.** DSpark uses DFlash as its parallel backbone with one change: the anchor itself is the first prediction position, so $\gamma$ input tokens (anchor $+$ $\gamma{-}1$ masks) yield $\gamma$ draft logits.[^chengDSparkConfidenceScheduledSpeculative2026]
- **M-DFlash.** Multiple-anchor-token DFlash, which extends DFlash from a single anchor to multiple feature-less anchors. It adds no new trainable network parameters and is initialized from the official DSpark checkpoint.[^liuWhenParallelDrafter2026]

The warm start initializes the body from a diffusion LM ([IDLM](i-dlm.md)) on a four-layer LayerSkip-Llama backbone. Cells are mean accepted length.[^bieOspreyTargetagnosticPretraining2026]

| Eval domain | Original DFlash, random body | Original DFlash, IDLM warm start | EAGLE-style DFlash, IDLM warm start |
|---|---|---|---|
| math (in-domain) | 5.96 | 6.03 | 5.25 |
| OOD average | 1.64 | 1.68 | 2.00 |

All rows: [^bieOspreyTargetagnosticPretraining2026]

- Under the original interface the warm start gains at most $0.07$ accepted tokens in any domain. The authors' hypothesis is key/value distribution drift: with [KV injection](../methods/kv-injection.md), the pretrained body's attention receives target-derived key/value streams it never saw in pretraining.[^bieOspreyTargetagnosticPretraining2026]
- The EAGLE-style variant adds zero-initialized target-side projections to $Q$, $K$, and $V$ and uses causal intra-block attention with shift-by-one labels.[^bieOspreyTargetagnosticPretraining2026]

### HyCo paper ([Bae et al. 2026](../papers/baeHyCoHybridDrafting2026.md)): server-side head for a cross-family verifier (Appendix H)

In additional runs for Muse-Glimmer-30B (NVFP4), Glimmer's own DFlash2 head (2.8B parameters), which reads five of the verifier's layers, drafted eight-token chains on the server, and HyCo's Gemma 4 E2B SLM Drafter on a Galaxy Z Fold8 added branches through a token bridge without retraining. Over all 480 Spec-Bench questions, a 32-node tree of the chain and a single SLM pass reached MAT 4.07, against 3.16 for a SpecEdge-style tree of four SLM passes and 127 nodes. HyCo notes that heads reading more verifier layers, such as DFlash, can need a larger hand-off if moved to the device, and leaves a device-runnable head for such families to future work.[^baeHyCoHybridDrafting2026]

## Strengths & Limitations

- Drafting cost is one parallel pass, so a five-layer DFlash drafter generating 16 tokens has lower latency and higher acceptance length than EAGLE-3 generating 8 tokens.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- Large blocks can increase verification cost under compute-bound settings such as large batch sizes. Adaptive block-size scheduling is left to future work.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- Speedup falls as concurrency rises: on SGLang, Qwen3-4B Math500 goes from 4.8× at concurrency 1 to 2.9× at concurrency 32.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- The base drafter trained on 4K context degrades beyond 4K context and needs fine-tuning for long contexts.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]
- DFlash predicts each position independently, so its acceptance decays at later positions of the block, a mode the DSpark paper calls multi-modal collision.[^chengDSparkConfidenceScheduledSpeculative2026]
- Under the sequential draft-verify schedule, drafting stays on the critical path. Across both target models and all seven benchmarks of the DPara paper, the serial draft stage accounts for 24%–32% of the per-step latency of DFlash/DSpark-style parallel drafters.[^liuWhenParallelDrafter2026]

# Contradictions

**1. DFlash acceptance length at temperature 1.0 with block (draft) length 7.** Two papers report $\tau$ for the same target models, benchmarks, temperature, and length, and the values differ.

| Model | Reported by | GSM8K | MATH-500 | HumanEval | MBPP | MT-Bench | Alpaca |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B | Cheng et al. | 5.33 | 4.91 | 4.64 | 4.36 | 3.11 | 2.98 |
| Qwen3-8B | Liu et al. | 5.28 | 5.15 | 4.64 | 4.47 | 3.18 | 2.98 |
| Qwen3-14B | Cheng et al. | 5.41 | 4.84 | 4.59 | 4.44 | 3.10 | 2.94 |
| Qwen3-14B | Liu et al. | 5.35 | 5.04 | 4.61 | 4.53 | 3.19 | 2.96 |

Rows "Cheng et al.": [^chengDSparkConfidenceScheduledSpeculative2026] Rows "Liu et al.": [^liuWhenParallelDrafter2026]

Setting differences: Cheng et al. retrain DFlash themselves and call the length "block size (7)".[^chengDSparkConfidenceScheduledSpeculative2026] Liu et al. call it "draft length 7", evaluate 80 requests per benchmark with up to 512 new tokens, and do not state the DFlash checkpoint.[^liuWhenParallelDrafter2026]

**2. Original numbers against the re-measurements.** For Qwen3-8B the original paper reports higher values than either later paper, under a different setting (its own checkpoint, block size 16, maximum of 2048 generated tokens, H200).[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026]

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| greedy, block size 16 | Chen et al. | 5.15× / 6.54 | 6.08× / 7.87 | 5.14× / 6.50 | 4.65× / 5.95 | 2.75× / 4.24 |
| greedy, draft length 7 | Liu et al. | 3.38× / 6.08 | 3.28× / 5.88 | 2.93× / 5.25 | 2.80× / 4.95 | 1.94× / 3.56 |
| temperature 1, block size 16 ($\tau$ only) | Chen et al. | 5.98 | 6.40 | 5.52 | 5.21 | 3.80 |
| temperature 1.0, block size 7 ($\tau$ only) | Cheng et al. | 5.33 | 4.91 | 4.64 | 4.36 | 3.11 |
| temperature 1.0, draft length 7 ($\tau$ only) | Liu et al. | 5.28 | 5.15 | 4.64 | 4.47 | 3.18 |
| greedy, draft length 16, vLLM, A100-40GB | An et al. (PARD-2) | 4.64 / 5.39 | 5.59 / 6.88 | 5.66 / 6.94 | not measured | 2.57 / 3.10 |

Rows "Chen et al.": [^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026] Rows "Cheng et al.": [^chengDSparkConfidenceScheduledSpeculative2026] Rows "Liu et al.": [^liuWhenParallelDrafter2026] Row "An et al.": [^anPARD2TargetAlignedParallel2026]

These rows are not a like-for-like conflict, because block size, checkpoint, generation length, and hardware all differ. They show that the headline speedups of the original paper are not reproduced at draft length 7. At draft length 16 on vLLM, PARD-2's measurement is lower than the original on GSM8K, MATH-500, and MT-Bench, but higher on HumanEval (5.66 / 6.94 against 5.14 / 6.50). The MT-Bench $\tau$ differs most (3.10 against 4.24).

**3. The margin over EAGLE-3.** The original paper reports that DFlash consistently outperforms EAGLE-3, with EAGLE-3 acceptance lengths near 3 on Qwen3 (for example Qwen3-8B GSM8K at temperature 1: EAGLE-3 (16) 3.12, EAGLE-3 (60) 3.59, DFlash 5.98).[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026] In the DSpark paper, with all drafters retrained on the same data, the Eagle3 TTT horizon (7) aligned with the block size (7), and chain-based drafting, Eagle3 reaches 5.30 against 5.33 for DFlash on Qwen3-8B GSM8K, and on Gemma4-12B Eagle3 is above DFlash in every column.[^chengDSparkConfidenceScheduledSpeculative2026] The DPara paper's EAGLE3 rows are close to the original paper's (Qwen3-8B, greedy: average 1.44× / 2.87).[^liuWhenParallelDrafter2026] Setting differences: the original paper uses EAGLE-3 checkpoints released by AngelSlim with 7 draft steps, top-$k$ 10, and draft trees of 16 or 60 tokens, while the DSpark paper retrains a 1-layer Eagle3 and uses a chain.[^chenDFlashBlockDiffusion2026][^chengDSparkConfidenceScheduledSpeculative2026]
