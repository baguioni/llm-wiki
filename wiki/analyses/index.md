# Analyses


* [DFlash, DSpark, and EAGLE-3: how they draft and what they report on Qwen3-8B](dflash-dspark-eagle-3-drafting-comparison.md) - Comparison of the drafting mechanism of EAGLE-3, DFlash, and DSpark, with every speedup and acceptance length reported for Qwen3-8B and the settings behind each number.
* [Applying the speculative drafter papers to a mobile phone NPU](speculative-drafter-papers-on-mobile-npu.md) - How the thirteen papers in the speculative drafter collection could apply to speculative decoding on a phone NPU, for an on-device target and for a phone drafter with a server verifier, read against the mobile NPU study and the on-phone measurements of Lever.
* [DFlash research directions for mobile SpecEdge](dflash-research-directions-for-mobile-specedge.md) - Which changes to a DFlash-style block drafter look worth testing for mobile-server speculative decoding, read from the drafter papers and my phone NPU profiling.
* [Choosing what to verify: Learning to Draft vs. DSpark](choosing-what-to-verify-ltd-vs-dspark.md) - How Learning to Draft picks draft tree depth and verification size with two RL policies, how DSpark picks a per-request chain prefix with calibrated confidence and a profiled throughput curve, and where the two differ.
* [Drafter training data across papers](drafter-training-data-across-papers.md) - Which datasets the speculative drafter papers train on, how many samples, whether the answers are regenerated by the target, and for how many epochs, with full recipes at about 0.5M to 1.4M samples and adaptation or ablation runs at 65K to 100K.
* [What the target must supply to train a drafter](target-signals-for-drafter-training.md) - Per drafter, which target outputs training needs (regenerated tokens, per-token target probabilities, the full target distribution, or hidden states from 3 to 5 layers), how papers obtain them online or offline, and what they cost to store for a Qwen3-14B target.
* [Why parallel drafters beat EAGLE-3](why-parallel-drafters-beat-eagle-3.md) - At equal depth an autoregressive drafter accepts more tokens, but one pass per block makes depth cheap and KV injection makes it useful, so a 5-layer DFlash beats a 1-layer EAGLE-3 on first-token acceptance and on wall-clock speedup.
* [Presenting the drafter evaluation the way the drafter papers do](presenting-the-drafter-evaluation.md) - How the DFlash, PARD, PARD-2, DSpark, and EAGLE-3 papers lay out their results in tables and figures, and a table-versus-figure plan for my six-drafter phone evaluation built on those conventions.
* [Building a parallel drafter for on-device use without training it from scratch](building-parallel-drafters-for-on-device-use.md) - Pruning a target gives an autoregressive model, but PARD shows that turning a small autoregressive model into a parallel drafter is a cheap fine-tune, PARD-2 shows that target features can enter through the input embedding of that narrow model, and on my phone such a 409 MB drafter matches the 1741 MB DFlash; the open step is to start from a pruned target.
* [Target-aware recovery of compressed drafters](target-aware-recovery-of-compressed-drafters.md) - Which speculative decoding papers recover a pruned or truncated drafter by training it against the target; HAT distills an adapter from the target's removed middle layers, Osprey recovers target-agnostically and then distills the target, Yan et al. distill without naming the teacher, and SDFP does no recovery.
* [Where Osprey's ideas fit a pruned-target PARD pipeline](osprey-ideas-for-pruned-target-pard-pipeline.md) - Maps Osprey's four stages onto my pipeline of Minitron pruning of the target, off-policy KD recovery, and PARD fine-tuning; zero-initialized feature injection and recovery-budget checks transfer directly, a pretrained small-LM source is an alternative first step, and vocabulary alignment, TTT, and cross-target reuse mostly do not apply.
* [Papers that train drafters for target agreement](drafter-training-for-target-agreement.md) - Which papers train a drafter to agree with its target rather than to be a better language model, and with what method (target-aimed KD, selective KD, acceptance losses, on-policy data, target-regenerated answers, online RL), plus the compressed-drafter papers, Osprey's counterpoint, and what pure TV versus the LK hybrid means for my pruned-target recovery.
* [Quantization of the target and the draft in speculative decoding](quantization-in-speculative-decoding.md) - What the wiki's sources say about 4-bit and 8-bit quantization in speculative decoding; a quantized target is faster but is a different model, which also shrinks the best draft budget, while a quantized draft loses only a few percent of acceptance (Q4_0 against fp16/bf16 in my runs; 4-bit QAT against BF16 in HyCo) and is much cheaper to run.
* [Training budgets for pruning recovery and drafter distillation](recovery-and-distillation-training-budgets.md) - How long the papers train to recover a pruned model or to distill a drafter; general-LM recovery runs for 50B to 94B tokens with no epoch count, drafter recoveries range from 1M tokens to about 462B, DistillSpec and GKD give 40K to 300K steps, and AdaSPEC trains 3 epochs as standard and 6 to 30 at its tuned optimum.
