# Papers that train drafters for target agreement

## Question

In [my pipeline](../experiments/minitron-pard-qwen3-14b.md), Qwen3-14B is pruned to the Qwen3-0.6B shape (stage 0) and then recovered with chat-KD. The goal of recovery is to raise the drafter's agreement with the target, not to regain general language ability. Which papers support that goal, and what methods do they use?

## Answer

Several drafter papers set acceptance, meaning agreement with the target, as the training goal instead of the student's own task quality:

- **DistillSpec:** target-aimed KD with on-policy data.
- **AdaSPEC:** selective KD.
- **LK Losses:** losses aimed at acceptance.
- **PARD-2:** target-aligned weighting.
- **Aurora:** online RL from live traces.
- **EAGLE-3, SpecForge and PARD:** answers regenerated by the target. My chat-KD recovery data is this same idea.
- **GKD:** a general (not drafter-specific) on-policy KD method that DistillSpec builds on.

Among drafters built by compressing a model, only HAT recovers directly against the target. No paper in the wiki prunes the target itself and then recovers it against the target alone, which is what my pipeline does. That gap is tracked in [Does recovering a pruned drafter against the target beat recovering it against its parent?](../questions/target-aware-recovery-of-pruned-drafters.md).

Osprey is the main counterpoint. It restores general LM ability first and only then aligns to the target.

## Evidence

### Papers that set target agreement as the goal

| Paper | Supporting claim | Method |
|---|---|---|
| [DistillSpec](../papers/zhouDistillSpecImprovingSpeculative2024.md) | KD for a draft aims at a higher acceptance rate, not a better task score. Gains in speculative decoding and in downstream task performance are only weakly correlated, so a high-quality distilled model is not necessarily an effective draft.[^zhouDistillSpecImprovingSpeculative2024] | White-box KD from the target, on-policy (draft-generated) data, and the divergence (FKL, RKL, JSD, TVD) treated as a hyperparameter per task. $10-45\%$ speedups over standard SD.[^zhouDistillSpecImprovingSpeculative2024] |
| [AdaSPEC](../papers/huAdaSPECSelectiveKnowledge2025.md) | Minimizing KL over all tokens is misaligned with SD's goal of maximizing acceptance. A small draft cannot absorb the whole target distribution, so capacity spent on hard tokens is wasted.[^huAdaSPECSelectiveKnowledge2025] | Distill a reference copy of the draft with forward KL. Then distill the draft with forward KL only on the top 40% of tokens by $\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{draft}}-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Higher acceptance than DistillSpec on every task and pair tested, by up to 15%.[^huAdaSPECSelectiveKnowledge2025] |
| [LK Losses](../papers/samarinLKLossesDirect2026.md) | Acceptance is $\alpha=1-\operatorname{TV}(p,q)$, so maximizing acceptance is equivalent to minimizing TV, and other divergences are proxy objectives.[^samarinLKLossesDirect2026] | Hybrid $\lambda\cdot\operatorname{KL}(p\|q)+(1-\lambda)\cdot\operatorname{TV}(p,q)$ with $\lambda=\exp(-\eta\cdot\mathrm{sg}[\alpha])$, or the likelihood form $-\log\alpha$.[^samarinLKLossesDirect2026] |
| [GKD](../papers/agarwalOnPolicyDistillationLanguage2024.md) | Training on a fixed set of sequences mismatches the sequences the student generates at inference.[^agarwalOnPolicyDistillationLanguage2024] | Train on student-generated sequences labelled with the teacher's token-level probabilities, with a student-data fraction $\lambda$. Performance improves as the on-policy share grows, provided at least 25% of the data is on-policy.[^agarwalOnPolicyDistillationLanguage2024] General KD, not a drafter paper; its related work names DistillSpec as applying GKD to draft alignment.[^agarwalOnPolicyDistillationLanguage2024] |
| [PARD-2](../papers/anPARD2TargetAlignedParallel2026.md) | Target-aligned training of a parallel drafter.[^anPARD2TargetAlignedParallel2026] | A distillation term toward the target, plus CAT weighting by the target's cumulative prefix confidence. Average $\tau$ is 5.79 with CAT against 5.61 for the best fixed decay ($\gamma=0.8$).[^anPARD2TargetAlignedParallel2026] |
| [Aurora](../papers/wangWhenRLMeets2026.md) | Offline-trained speculators suffer delayed utility feedback and degrade under domain drift.[^wangWhenRLMeets2026] | Online learning from live inference traces as asynchronous RL: accepted tokens give positive feedback and rejected proposals implicit negative feedback. An additional 1.25× speedup over a well-trained static speculator.[^wangWhenRLMeets2026] |
| [EAGLE-3](../papers/liEAGLE3ScalingInference2025.md), [SpecForge](../papers/liSpecForgeFlexibleEfficient2026.md), [PARD](../papers/anPARDAcceleratingLLM2025.md) | Train on the target's own answers rather than the dataset's original answers. | EAGLE-3 regenerates answers with the target "rather than using a fixed dataset".[^liEAGLE3ScalingInference2025] In SpecForge, regenerating raises acceptance length on nearly all benchmarks and average throughput by 5.3%.[^liSpecForgeFlexibleEfficient2026] PARD's Qwen drafter trains on regenerated answers only.[^anPARDAcceleratingLLM2025] |

### Drafters built by compressing a model

| Paper | What the recovery aims at |
|---|---|
| [HAT](../papers/xieNovelHatShapedDeviceCloud2025.md) | The target. It keeps the target's first $m$ layers and head, and distills an adapter to imitate the removed middle layers.[^xieNovelHatShapedDeviceCloud2025] |
| [Osprey](../papers/bieOspreyTargetagnosticPretraining2026.md) | General LM first, then the target. Qwen3-4B is pruned to 2 blocks, pretrained on about 462B FineWeb tokens with no target, then adapted by distilling the target.[^bieOspreyTargetagnosticPretraining2026] |
| [SDFP](../papers/weiSDFPSpeculativeDecoding2026.md) | Nothing. Pruned target layers are used with no training.[^weiSDFPSpeculativeDecoding2026] |
| [Decoding Speculative Decoding](../papers/yanDecodingSpeculativeDecoding2025.md) | Distillation over 1 million tokens. The teacher is not named.[^yanDecodingSpeculativeDecoding2025] |
| [Minitron](../papers/muralidharanCompactLanguageModels2024.md), [Sheared LLaMA](../papers/xiaShearedLLaMAAccelerating2024.md) | General LM quality: distillation from the unpruned model,[^muralidharanCompactLanguageModels2024] or continued pre-training on 50B tokens.[^xiaShearedLLaMAAccelerating2024] Neither is a drafter paper. |

See [Target-aware recovery of compressed drafters](target-aware-recovery-of-compressed-drafters.md) for the details of each.

### The counterpoint: Osprey restores general ability first

- Osprey's drafters reach a mean AL of 3.686 on the Qwen3-8B 5×5 cross-domain grid, against 3.176 for a from-scratch EAGLE-3.[^bieOspreyTargetagnosticPretraining2026]
- A 10k-step pretraining checkpoint adapts to an overall AL of 3.552, against 3.578 for 55k steps.[^bieOspreyTargetagnosticPretraining2026]
- Its pruned model is not the target, and it has no arm that skips the general stage.[^bieOspreyTargetagnosticPretraining2026]

**Inference.** A little general recovery may help more than a lot of it does. My pruned drafter's weakest Spec-Bench task is translation, where it reaches 0.44 of Qwen3-0.6B's MAT at $\gamma=5$, against 0.65–0.74 on the other tasks.[^minitron-pard-qwen3-14b] That is the kind of coverage gap a general stage could fill. The other lever is broader target-generated data, which is the next step my report names (the Magpie pool has about 300K prompts).[^minitron-pard-qwen3-14b]

### My recovery fits this goal

- Chat-KD recovers the pruned Qwen3-14B with online forward KL from Qwen3-14B itself, on 49,372 of its own greedy answers, over the full vocabulary at temperature 1.0 and on answer tokens only.[^minitron-pard-qwen3-14b]
- An earlier recovery on SlimPajama web text is superseded. No SlimPajama loss is reported after recovery: the only values are 1.907 for Qwen3-14B and 9.60 for the pruned model at zero-shot.[^minitron-pard-qwen3-14b]
- On Spec-Bench, top-1 agreement with the target rises from 0.038 to 0.572 and MAT at $\gamma=5$ from 1.04 to 2.17, against 0.747 and 3.12 for Qwen3-0.6B.[^minitron-pard-qwen3-14b]

### Pure TV against the LK hybrid

These two are different objectives, and the evidence on them is different.

- **The LK hybrid.** The adaptive $\lambda$ keeps the loss close to KL while acceptance is low, and moves it toward TV as acceptance rises. The paper describes this as a trust region: minimize TV subject to $\operatorname{KL}(p\|q)\leq\delta$.[^samarinLKLossesDirect2026]
- **Pure TV from random initialization.** It fails because the TV gradient has norm $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{k}/V)$, which is tiny for vocabularies over 100k.[^samarinLKLossesDirect2026] For EAGLE-3 on Llama-3.1-8B at $T=0$, pure TV gives $\tau$ 2.81 / 3.42 / 3.34 on MT-Bench / HumanEval / GSM8K. KL gives 3.75 / 4.82 / 4.50, and the hybrid with $\eta=3$ gives 3.84 / 4.89 / 4.57.[^samarinLKLossesDirect2026]
- **AdaSPEC does not use the hybrid.** It uses forward KL only. Its ablation swaps in TVD or RKL as a single divergence, with the 40% token filter still on. Draft acceptance on GSM8K falls from 63.22% (forward KL) to 9.09% (TVD).[^huAdaSPECSelectiveKnowledge2025]
- **The evidence on pure TV conflicts.** In DistillSpec, TVD-based f-Distill is the best method in most of its Table C.1, with drafts initialized from supervised fine-tuned checkpoints and trained for 300,000 steps. Yet the same paper states that directly optimizing TVD does not give the best performance in most settings explored.[^zhouDistillSpecImprovingSpeculative2024] AdaSPEC attributes DistillSpec's TVD results to that long, costly schedule.[^huAdaSPECSelectiveKnowledge2025] The conflict is tracked in [Which divergence should a draft model be distilled with?](../questions/drafter-distillation-divergence.md).

**Inference.** Pure TV has worked only after a long schedule from a fine-tuned start. My recovery starts from stage 0, at held-out top-1 0.023 and forward KL 9.78, and runs 1,543 steps per epoch.[^minitron-pard-qwen3-14b] That is closer to the conditions where pure TV fails. So a TV arm for my recovery should use the hybrid, not the `tvd` loss alone. AdaSPEC's token selection and the LK hybrid change different things (which tokens are trained, and the divergence per token), so they could be combined. No paper in the wiki tests that combination.

## Caveats

- "Target agreement" is my framing. The papers speak of alignment, acceptance rate, or acceptance length.
- Most of these papers train drafters that are not compressed (DistillSpec, AdaSPEC, LK Losses, PARD-2, Aurora). They support the goal, not the pruned-target route.
- GKD measures task quality, not acceptance.[^agarwalOnPolicyDistillationLanguage2024]
- At $T=0$, the hybrid's mean gains over KL are small: +1.6% for EAGLE-3 on Llama-3.1-8B, +0.5% on Llama-3.3-70B and +0.9% on GPT-OSS 20B, in mean $\tau$. KL is ahead in two cells.[^samarinLKLossesDirect2026] My Spec-Bench numbers are greedy.[^minitron-pard-qwen3-14b]
- AdaSPEC's comparisons are against DistillSpec on small pairs (Pythia-31M → 1.4B, CodeGen-350M → Phi-2), plus one Qwen2.5-0.5B → 32B run on GSM8K.[^huAdaSPECSelectiveKnowledge2025]
